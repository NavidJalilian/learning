Earn extra signal: do the maths out loud. The book never computes traffic for this chapter. You can: 10M + 1M + 5M = 16 million notifications a day. Step 3 · "what happens when…?"

Now the interviewer stops nodding and starts breaking things. Seven curveballs. Each has three answers: a senior one (+XP), a junior one that sounds fine but misses something (no XP, no heart lost), and a wrong one (costs a heart).

Honest caveats from the curveballs. (1) Exactly-once delivery is impossible end to end over an unreliable network[3]. Say "at-least-once, with dedupe" instead. (2) The book doesn't cover the queue machines failing, priority queues, or switching providers mid-outage. Those answers come from general practice, not the chapter. 🎓 Level-up note

  • The numbers are yours to add. The book gives daily volumes but never turns them into traffic per second. Saying "about 185 a second on average, but very bursty" is our addition, and an easy way to earn signal.
  • Channel queues vs. priority queues. The book splits queues by channel only. Splitting by priority too (login codes vs. marketing) and replicating the queues are senior extensions, not book content.
  • Never promise exactly-once. Say "at-least-once, with dedupe by event ID". The dedupe check shrinks duplicates; a crash between sending and recording can still let one through.
  • Token hygiene is real work. APNs answers 410 when a device token is no longer valid[4], and Firebase recommends removing stale tokens[5]. The book doesn't mention either.
  • The clock is a game. The 45-minute limit and step timings follow the book's framework chapter. Real interview loops vary; many run 60 minutes.
