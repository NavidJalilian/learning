3

Dig in

Step 3 · frontier, downloader, robustness

The interviewer zooms in three times: the URL frontier (what to fetch next), the HTML downloader (how to fetch fast), and then pushback on choices a candidate usually makes. Each zoom opens when you finish the one before.

Honest caveats. (1) The two-layer frontier comes from Mercator, a late-1990s crawler[3]. Modern crawlers keep the skeleton but add per-IP and adaptive rate limits (many small sites share one server), and often machine-learned priorities. (2) robots.txt caching has a limit: the standard says a crawler shouldn't keep using a cached copy for more than 24 hours unless the file can't be fetched[6].
4

Boss: the curveball barrage

Step 3 · "what happens when…?"

The design is on the board. Now the interviewer starts breaking it. Ten "what happens when…?" questions. For each one, name which part of the design you'd fix first.

📋 Frontier: priority · politeness · freshness⬇️ Downloader: robots · DNS · locality · timeout🛡️ Robustness: hashing · checkpoints · exceptions🧹 Content: dedupe · traps · noise · render
Honest caveat. Real interviewers rarely fire ten curveballs. More often they pick one area and push hard, most often the frontier's politeness and priority. It's fine to skim the rest and go deep where they push. Also: the book's spider-trap advice (cap URL length, review odd sites by hand) is a start; real crawlers also cap pages per host and crawl depth.

🎓 Level-up note

Three places where the book's clean story meets real life. (1) It's a 1990s skeleton. Frontier, fetch, parse, dedupe, extract, filter, seen-check: that loop still holds. Modern crawlers add JavaScript rendering pipelines, near-duplicate fingerprints, per-IP and adaptive rate limits, and learned priorities. (2) Exact hashes are blunt. A copied article with a different date or ad block hashes differently, so Content Seen? misses it. Near-duplicate detection (shingling, SimHash) catches those[5]. (3) Know where the crawler ends. Indexing, ranking and serving search results read from Content Storage. Say they're downstream and out of scope, so the interviewer knows you know.
